# Issue-area mappings

These files assign every roll call, 1789–2027, to Clausen's six issue areas. Clausen
codes stop at congress 113 (2015); these crosswalks fill congresses 114–119 from the
Congressional Research Service (CRS) policy areas, which start at congress 93 (1973).
Generated by `python -m political_compass.issue_mappings` from Voteview's
`data/HSall_rollcalls.json` (see [data/README.md](../data/README.md)); do not edit by hand.

The six areas, in column order everywhere:
`Government Management`, `Foreign and Defense Policy`, `Social Welfare`,
`Civil Liberties`, `Agriculture`, `Miscellaneous Policy`.

## Files

**`crs_policy_area_to_clausen.csv`**: one row per CRS policy area (31).

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `crs_policy_area` | CRS policy area, exactly as in Voteview's `crs_policy_area` field |
| `n_overlap` | roll calls in congresses 93–113 carrying both this area and a Clausen code |
| `clausen_majority` | the Clausen area those roll calls most often got (the hard mapping) |
| `majority_share` | share of them in `clausen_majority` |
| `stable_across_halves` | `False` if fitting on congresses 93–103 vs 104–113 gives a different majority |
| six area columns | share in each Clausen area (the soft mapping; rows sum to 1 within rounding) |

**`vote_kind_to_clausen.csv`**: for roll calls with no policy area, one row per kind.

| `vote_kind` | `bill_number_match` | Clausen mix it gets |
|---|---|---|
| `nomination` | `regex:^PN` | 87% Government Management |
| `treaty` | `regex:^TREATYDOC` | 98% Foreign and Defense Policy |
| `no bill number` | `missing` | 63% Miscellaneous Policy |
| `bill without area` | `otherwise` | the mix of bill votes whose area is unknown; a weak prior (43% Government Management) |

Same remaining columns as above. Kinds are tested in table order.

## Lookup order

```python
import pandas as pd

from political_compass import issue_mappings as im

areas = pd.read_csv("mappings/crs_policy_area_to_clausen.csv", index_col="crs_policy_area")
kinds = pd.read_csv("mappings/vote_kind_to_clausen.csv", index_col="vote_kind")
rc = im.load_rollcalls()  # congress, chamber, rollnumber, bill_number, clausen, crs_policy_area

# 1. congresses 1-113: the roll call's own Clausen code
w = pd.get_dummies(rc["clausen"]).reindex(columns=im.CLAUSEN_AREAS, fill_value=0).astype(float)
todo = w.sum(axis=1) == 0
# 2. otherwise its policy area (its own, or borrowed from the same bill's other roll calls)
area = im.borrow_bill_area(rc, rc)
by_area = todo & area.isin(areas.index)
w.loc[by_area] = areas.loc[area[by_area], im.CLAUSEN_AREAS].to_numpy()
# 3. otherwise the kind of vote (nomination, treaty, no bill number, bill without area)
rest = todo & ~by_area
w.loc[rest] = kinds.loc[im.vote_kind(rc.loc[rest, "bill_number"]), im.CLAUSEN_AREAS].to_numpy()
```

`w` then holds one row of six weights per roll call. For a single label per roll call,
use `clausen_majority` instead of the weights in steps 2–3, or take `w.idxmax(axis=1)`.

## How they were built

- **Learned from the overlap.** In congresses 93–113, 25,016 roll calls carry both a
  CRS policy area and a Clausen code. Each area's weights are the shares of its roll
  calls in each Clausen area. The vote-kind weights come from the overlap roll calls
  that have no policy area, even after borrowing from the same bill.
- **Only roll calls the pipeline scales are used.** Near-unanimous votes are excluded
  (`data_io.contested_rollcalls`), so the weights describe the votes that get
  interpreted. Borrowing a bill's area may draw on any of its roll calls.
- **NOMINATE fields are never read.**

## How good they are

- **Hard mapping:** agrees with the actual Clausen code on 74.7% of overlap roll calls.
  Fitted on congresses 93–103 and tested on 104–113, it agrees on 74.2%. A policy
  area labels the whole bill, while Clausen labels each vote, and amendments to an
  appropriations bill can be about anything.
- **Use the weights for shares.** The hard mapping skews the mix, while the weights
  reproduce it by construction:

  | Clausen area | actual | hard mapping | weights |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Government Management | 53.3% | 69.4% | 53.3% |
  | Foreign and Defense Policy | 18.6% | 15.6% | 18.6% |
  | Social Welfare | 15.0% | 9.4% | 15.0% |
  | Civil Liberties | 6.3% | 3.1% | 6.3% |
  | Agriculture | 3.4% | 2.4% | 3.4% |
  | Miscellaneous Policy | 3.4% | 0.0% | 3.4% |

- **Unclear areas** (`majority_share` near 0.5 or `stable_across_halves` false):
  - Foreign Trade and International Finance: 49% Foreign and Defense vs 45% Government Management.
  - Housing and Community Development: 55% Government Management vs 42% Social Welfare.
  - Emergency Management: unstable between the two halves.
  - Law: 46% majority.
  - Congress: 52% majority.
  - Sports and Recreation: only 25 roll calls.
- **Where congresses 114–119 get their weights**, out of 9,509 scaled roll calls:
  - own policy area: 33%
  - borrowed from the same bill: 26%
  - nominations: 24%
  - no bill number: 2%
  - `bill without area`: 15% (a prior, not information about the vote)
- **Composition of congresses 114–119.** Government Management is higher there than in
  1981–2015 (59% vs 50% by weight), mostly because nominations make up a quarter
  of modern votes.
- **Not mapped:** "Social Sciences and History" appears only on near-unanimous votes
  and never after 2015, so it has no row. The builder warns if a post-2015 area is
  missing from the mapping.
